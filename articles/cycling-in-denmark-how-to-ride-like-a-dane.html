Cycling in Denmark: How to ride like a Dane

October 24, 2025 • 10 min read • Back to articles
Denmark cycling travel

In Denmark, bicycles are everywhere, in every kind of weather and at every hour of the day. For many Danes, cycling isn't just transportation, it's a lifestyle, a statement, even part of their personality. Thanks to excellent infrastructure, bike-friendly laws, and countless repair shops, Denmark is one of the best places in the world to ride. It would be a shame not to explore downtown Copenhagen or the peaceful countryside on two wheels.

Essential Bike Rules

  • Always stay on the right side of bike paths.
  • Use the left side only to overtake slower cyclists.
  • To turn left at a junction, go straight across first, stop at the corner, and wait for the next green light, known as the Copenhagen left turn.
  • Your bike must have a bell to warn others.
  • Always keep at least one hand on the handlebars to steer safely.
  • It's illegal to use a handheld mobile phone while cycling. Stop on the sidewalk if you need to check your map.
  • When it gets dark, indicated by the streetlights turning on, you need a white front light, a red rear light, and reflectors on your bike.
  • Don't bike if you're too drunk. If you're a danger to yourself or others, the police can fine you up to 1,500 kr. Take a train, metro, or walk home safely instead.

Cycling Etiquette

  • Signal turns by extending your left or right arm. Always check for cars and other cyclists first.
  • Raise your hand slightly to signal that you're stopping, and don't brake suddenly, especially during rush hour.
  • Don't block intersections or bike paths when you stop. Move to the sidewalk instead.
  • Wear a helmet. It's not required by law, but your head will thank you in an accident.
  • Avoid wearing headphones while biking. You need to hear cars, cyclists, and pedestrians around you.

Practical Tips

  • Danes bike year-round, in rain, snow, or shine. Invest in a good raincoat, waterproof pants, and gloves.
  • Use a phone mount or cycling computer if you need navigation. Set it up before you start riding.
  • Denmark can be very windy. Keep both hands on the handlebars, as strong gusts can knock you off balance.
  • Buy a solid bike lock. Theft happens, especially in Copenhagen and Aarhus.
  • Learn how to lock your bike properly: secure the frame (the triangle part) to something sturdy, not just the wheel. It won't make your bike theft-proof, but it will make it harder to steal.
  • If you have an expensive bike, take it indoors and get insurance. Register it with the police and use the lock type your insurer recommends.
  • Many bike shops offer free air pumps outside. Keep an eye out for them, as they are lifesavers.
  • Learn basic bike maintenance. Being able to fix a flat tire or adjust your brakes will save you time and money.

If you don't own a bike, try the white Bycyklen electric city bikes. You can rent and unlock them with an app, and they're available all over Copenhagen. The city even has dedicated bike bridges like Cykelslangen ("The Cycle Snake") and its own traffic lights for cyclists.

There will always be a few Danes who ignore the rules, but it's smarter and safer to ride responsibly. Whether you're gliding through the morning mist over the lakes or battling headwinds down Nørrebrogade, cycling here isn't just about getting from A to B. It's part of living like a Dane.
